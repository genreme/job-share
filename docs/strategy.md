## 1. Job Search Strategy & Decision Framework

### Why the 7-Category Fit Scoring System?

The 7-category fit scoring system was developed to **systematically evaluate opportunities** rather than relying on gut feel or single factors like salary. At the executive level, job satisfaction depends on multiple dimensions working together - a great salary can't compensate for mission misalignment, and a perfect mission can't justify a role that doesn't match your experience level.

The system provides:
- **Objective comparison** across diverse opportunities (nonprofit vs for-profit, local vs remote, etc.)
- **Structured decision-making** that prevents impulse applications
- **Clear communication** with partners/advisors about why certain roles matter
- **Pattern recognition** over time about what actually predicts satisfaction

### Category Weights & Rationale

**Total: 100 points**

| Category | Weight | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **Mission Alignment** | 20 points | Top priority - you're coming from 9 years at a mission-driven org and consistently mention this as non-negotiable. Healthcare, education, arts/culture, social impact sectors score highest. |
| **Location** | 20 points | Family with toddler in East Boston since 2019, deep community ties. Boston/MA strongly preferred, remote acceptable, relocation unlikely. |
| **Role Fit** | 20 points | Must match experience level (Director+), involve both strategic leadership AND hands-on creative work, ideally building/transforming creative infrastructure. |
| **Salary** | 20 points | Floor: $120K (below this is not viable). Target: $145K-$165K. Stretch: $170K+. Total comp matters more than base at this stage. |
| **Growth Potential** | 15 points | Opportunity to expand skills (AI, new sectors), build something from scratch or transform existing, clear advancement path or increased scope. |
| **Culture Fit** | 15 points | Team-first culture, sustainable pace (not agency burnout), values systems thinking, respects creative voice in strategy, diverse perspectives. |
| **Industry** | 10 points | Bonus points for education, healthcare, arts/music, travel. Less critical than mission but helps with passion and network leverage. |

**Why these weights:**
- Mission + Location + Role Fit = 60% → These are "must-haves" that can't be compromised
- Salary + Growth + Culture = 50% → These are "optimize-fors" where trade-offs are acceptable
- Industry = 10% → Nice-to-have differentiator but mission matters more

### Decision Thresholds

**Apply Now (85-100):** Full process - customized resume, strategic cover letter, networking outreach  
**Maybe (70-84):** Quick application or wait for better timing - templated materials acceptable  
**Applied:** Already submitted - track for follow-up  
**Probably Not (<70):** Skip unless strategic reason (networking, learn about sector, referral bonus)  
**Archived:** Closed, rejected, or withdrawn

## 3. Networking Strategy

### MSU Alumni Approach

**Context:** Michigan State University undergrad (2012-2016), strong alumni network in Boston area

**Strategy:**
- Leverage for **informational conversations**, not direct job asks
- Frame as peer-to-peer learning: "I'm at PIH, you're at [TARGET], curious about creative work in [SECTOR]"
- Best for: Higher ed, healthcare, nonprofit sector (MSU grads often mission-driven)
- Approach: "Hi [NAME], fellow Spartan here - saw you're at [COMPANY]. I'm exploring opportunities in [SECTOR] and would love to learn about your experience there. Free for a quick call?"

**Not recommended:** Cold outreach to MSU alumni at for-profit agencies (feels transactional)

### LinkedIn Connection Strategy

**Passive Recruiter Attraction (Primary Strategy):**

**Profile Optimization:**
- Headline: "VP Creative | Creative Operations Leader | Scaling Mission-Driven Creative Teams" (220 char)
- Open to Work: Recruiters Only (not public)
- Job titles: Creative Director, VP Creative, Head of Design, Director of Creative Services, Executive Creative Director
- Skills prioritized: Creative Operations (differentiator), Creative Direction, Brand Development, Design Leadership, Team Leadership

**Active Outreach (When Targeting Specific Job):**

**Hierarchy of Connections:**
1. **Strong referral** → Ask for direct intro to hiring manager
2. **Weak connection** → Rebuild relationship first, don't lead with job ask
3. **Can identify HM** → Lead with insight/observation, not job ask
4. **Can't identify HM** → Informational approach to someone in department

**Message Templates:**

**For Warm Intro Request (to referrer):**
> "Hey [REFERRER] - wanted to connect you with [HM NAME]. I spent almost a decade leading creative at Partners In Health [BRIEF ACCOMPLISHMENT]. Exploring opportunities in [SECTOR] and saw the [ROLE] at [COMPANY]. Thought it might be worth connecting since [RELEVANT CONTEXT]. Let me know if you're open to a quick intro."

**For Cold Outreach to Hiring Manager:**
> "Hi [HM NAME] - I've been following [COMPANY]'s work on [SPECIFIC PROJECT/INITIATIVE]. As someone who [BRIEF CREDIBILITY], I'm genuinely curious about [SPECIFIC QUESTION ABOUT THEIR WORK]. Would you be open to a brief conversation?"

**Rules:**
- Never mention job in initial outreach (too transactional)
- Lead with genuine observation or question
- Keep under 300 characters for connection requests
- Follow up only if they respond (no double-messaging)

### Warm Intro Tracking

**System:** Track in dashboard "Updates" log
- Who introduced you
- Date of intro
- Outcome (conversation scheduled, no response, etc.)
- Follow-up actions needed

**Best Practices:**
- Thank referrer regardless of outcome
- Keep referrer updated (brief note when you get interview, offer, etc.)
- Never burn bridge with referrer by ghosting their contact
- If intro doesn't work out, still thank the contact for their time

**Example Updates Log:**
- "Sarah Dunlap (PIH colleague) intro to Media Cause - interviewed with Katey & Ryan, ultimately rejected but great experience"
- "Amy Carzo intro to Tufts MJ Kim - connected on LinkedIn, applied through website"

## 4. Salary & Negotiation Framework

### Your Numbers

**Floor (Walk-Away):** $120K base  
- Minimum viable for family in Boston
- Below this, role must be exceptional in all other categories to consider

**Target Range:** $145K-$165K base  
- ~30-40% increase from PIH nonprofit comp (~$110K-$130K estimated)
- Market rate for Director/VP Creative at mission-driven orgs in Boston
- Comfortable living standard without overreach

**Stretch (If Exceptional Fit):** $170K-$190K+ base  
- For-profit companies or well-funded nonprofits
- Roles with significant scope (large teams, multiple departments)
- Total comp packages where bonus/equity add substantial value

### Market Research (By Sector)

**Nonprofit/Higher Ed:**
- Creative Director: $110K-$150K
- VP Creative: $140K-$180K
- Senior Director: $120K-$160K

**Healthcare (Hospitals/Systems):**
- Director Creative Services: $130K-$170K
- VP Brand & Creative: $160K-$200K+
- Manager-level: $90K-$120K

**EdTech/For-Profit Education:**
- Creative Director: $140K-$180K
- VP Creative: $170K-$220K
- Below market at some (Lexia = $140K-$155K reality)

**Agencies (Nonprofit/Social Impact):**
- VP Creative: $150K-$190K
- Creative Director: $130K-$170K
- Often below corporate but above nonprofit

**Museums/Cultural Institutions:**
- Director-level: $100K-$140K
- VP-level: $130K-$170K
- Often constrained budgets, negotiate on title/scope/flexibility

### Negotiation Talking Points

**Your Value Proposition:**
- 9+ years progressive experience (solo → 10-person team + 25 freelancers)
- Built enterprise creative infrastructure (75-component design system, 250K-asset DAM)
- Proven ROI: Digital fundraising +52% ($11M → $16.7M), org revenue +91% ($131M → $250M)
- Award-winning work: IDA Silver, PINE Pinnacle, Dr. Paul Farmer portrait (NYT, PBS, Congress)
- 80% team retention over 7 years (rare in creative)
- Global operations across 11 countries (complex stakeholder management)

**Deflection Techniques:**

**"What are you making now?"**
❌ "I make $118K at PIH"  
✅ "I'm currently at a nonprofit where compensation structures differ from [FOR-PROFIT/CORPORATE]. I'm looking for market rate for this role, which I understand is $[TARGET RANGE] in [LOCATION]. What's budgeted for this position?"

**"What's your salary expectation?"**
❌ "I'm flexible" / "Whatever you think is fair"  
✅ "Based on my research for [ROLE LEVEL] roles in [LOCATION/SECTOR], I'm targeting $[TARGET]-$[TARGET+20K], but I'm open to discussing the full package including benefits, bonus, and growth trajectory. What range has [COMPANY] budgeted?"

**"That's higher than we expected"**
❌ "Oh, I can go lower"  
✅ "I understand budget constraints [IF NONPROFIT: especially in this sector]. I'm flexible on structure - could we explore options like sign-on bonus, performance bonus, earlier comp review, or additional benefits? I'm most interested in the right total package and opportunity."

### Total Compensation Levers

**If base salary is constrained, negotiate:**

1. **Performance Bonus:** 10-20% target (adds $15K-$30K annually)
2. **Sign-On Bonus:** $10K-$25K (bridges gap in year one)
3. **Equity/Stock Options:** If applicable (startups, for-profits)
4. **Professional Development:** $3K-$5K annual budget
5. **Remote Work Stipend:** $1K-$2K for home office
6. **Additional PTO:** 5 extra days (worth ~$3K-$5K)
7. **Flexible Schedule:** 4-day week or compressed schedule (no cost to them)
8. **Title Elevation:** "Senior" or "Executive" prefix (future leverage)
9. **Earlier Comp Review:** 6-month vs 12-month first review
10. **Relocation Assistance:** If considering move

**Example Trade:**
> "If base is fixed at $145K, could we structure it as: $145K base + $15K sign-on + 15% bonus target ($21K) + $3K prof dev = $184K year one, $169K ongoing?"

### Red Flags in Salary Discussions

🚩 **"We don't negotiate"** → Walk away or push back ("Total comp includes more than base")  
🚩 **"You'll get a raise after proving yourself"** → Get it in writing with specific metrics/timeline  
🚩 **"Below market but great culture/mission"** → Be honest about trade-off ("I need $X minimum to make this work")  
🚩 **"What's your current salary?"** → Deflect to market rate, don't anchor low  
🚩 **"That's way above our range"** → Clarify their actual range ("What IS your range?")  

### Decision Framework

**Accept if:**
- Base ≥ $145K OR base ≥ $140K with strong total comp package
- Mission + role fit + culture all score 15+ in fit scoring system
- Total opportunity score ≥ 85 (Apply Now tier)

**Negotiate if:**
- Offer is $130K-$145K base (push toward $145K-$155K)
- Good fit but salary below target
- Strong non-salary benefits can close gap

**Decline if:**
- Base < $120K with no room to negotiate
- Significant red flags in culture, role scope, or mission alignment
- Better opportunities in pipeline
- Gut says this isn't right (trust instinct even if numbers work)

**Remember:** You're negotiating from strength (9+ years experience, proven results, in-demand skills). Never apologize for your worth.

## 5. Job Board Effectiveness Analysis

### Tier 1: Focus Here (Proven ROI)

**Lever (lever.co)**
- **Why it works:** Premium companies use it, clean UX, good email alerts
- **Best for:** Startups, tech-adjacent nonprofits, modern mission-driven orgs
- **Frequency:** Daily check or set up alerts for "Creative Director" + "Boston"

**Greenhouse (greenhouse.io job boards)**
- **Why it works:** Many mission-driven orgs use it, good filtering, active postings
- **Best for:** Nonprofits, education, healthcare, cultural institutions
- **How to use:** Search "[company].greenhouse.io" directly or find via company website
- **Example:** Partners In Health, Museum of Science, many universities use Greenhouse

**Foundation List (foundationlist.com)**
- **Why it works:** Curated nonprofit/foundation roles, mission-aligned
- **Best for:** Development/communications roles at foundations and large nonprofits
- **Frequency:** Weekly check, roles stay open longer

### Tier 2: Monitor Selectively

**Built In Boston (builtinboston.com)**
- **Why it works:** Boston-specific, tech + mission-driven companies, good for culture research
- **Best for:** Boston-based companies, understanding comp ranges, culture intel
- **Limitation:** Leans tech/startup, fewer pure creative roles
- **How to use:** Weekly check, use for company research even if not applying through platform

**LinkedIn Jobs**
- **Why it works:** Volume, easy apply, recruiter visibility
- **Best for:** Passive applications, seeing what's out there, networking context
- **Limitation:** Lots of noise, reposted stale jobs, easy apply = high competition
- **How to use:** Set alerts for saved searches, but always apply via company website when possible

**Workday**
- **Why it works:** Many large organizations use it (universities, hospitals, large nonprofits)
- **Best for:** Higher ed, healthcare systems, enterprise nonprofits
- **Limitation:** Clunky UX, slow ATS, can't save progress easily
- **How to use:** Expect 30-45 min application, have all materials ready before starting

### Tier 3: Skip or Minimal Time

**Indeed**
- **Why skip:** Stale postings, scammy listings, optimized for volume not quality
- **Exception:** Set alerts ONLY for very specific searches ("Creative Director" + "Nonprofit" + "Boston")
- **Never:** Browse or spend significant time here

**Glassdoor**
- **Why skip for job search:** Mostly reposts from other sites, login wall issues
- **Use instead for:** Salary research, company culture intel, interview prep
- **Don't:** Apply through Glassdoor, use it only for research

**Idealist (idealist.org)**
- **Why skip:** Listings often 30-60+ days old, design hasn't updated since 2010, mostly entry-level
- **Was useful:** 5-10 years ago, now legacy platform
- **Exception:** Very specific niche nonprofit roles in international development

**JazzHR, BambooHR, iCIMS**
- **Why skip:** SMB-focused ATS systems, usually smaller orgs with less competitive comp
- **Signal:** If company uses these, probably not big enough for Director+ creative role
- **Exception:** If referred directly to role at small org, tolerate the bad UX

### Dropped Completely

**ZipRecruiter** - Mostly spam, recruiter farms, low-quality postings  
**Monster** - Outdated, irrelevant roles for your level  
**CareerBuilder** - Same as Monster, mostly corporate ladder roles  
**Craigslist** - Occasionally gems but 99% noise, not worth time

## 6. Interview Insights

### What We Learned from Media Cause Process

**Context:** VP of Creative role at nonprofit marketing agency, 4-interview process (Katey Parker CSO, Ryan Fuquea EVP Client Services, Eric Facas CEO, Ellen Corrigan Chief Growth Officer), ultimately rejected but valuable experience

#### What Worked

**1. Systems Thinking Resonated**
- Emphasized building design systems, DAM, workflows at PIH
- Positioned creative as infrastructure, not just output
- Directly addressed their stated need for "processes and team resourcing"
- **Lesson:** At Director+ level, process/systems expertise matters as much as creative vision

**2. Data-Driven Creative Approach**
- Discussed dashboards, A/B testing, immediate pivoting based on results
- Matched their emphasis on "data matters probably the most"
- **Lesson:** Mission-driven ≠ ignoring metrics, show you optimize for impact

**3. Proactive AI Positioning**
- Brought up AI before they asked, discussed ethics/boundaries
- Showed sophistication (facial recognition concerns, background expansion use cases)
- **Lesson:** Don't wait to be asked about emerging tech, demonstrate you're already there

**4. Service-Oriented Leadership**
- "Creative as service to the organization" language
- Win-win collaboration framing
- Directly countered previous leader's siloed approach
- **Lesson:** Humility beats ego at mission-driven orgs

**5. Question About Previous Leader's Legacy**
- Asked what worked/didn't work with Amy Small's tenure
- Drew out critical intelligence about why role was open
- Showed strategic thinking about organizational context
- **Lesson:** Ask about predecessor, signals you understand roles have history

**6. Specific Metrics Ready**
- 80% retention, 52% fundraising growth, 30% faster delivery
- Made abstract leadership concrete
- **Lesson:** Know your numbers cold, use them strategically

#### What Didn't Work / Could Improve

**1. Didn't Address Nonprofit-to-Agency Transition Enough**
- Assumed nonprofit experience would translate obviously
- Should have explicitly discussed pace, client service, new business context
- **Lesson:** Name the transition, don't make them wonder if you understand the difference

**2. Could Have Been More Concrete on New Business Pitches**
- They emphasized this need multiple times ("show rather than tell")
- Gave general answer about presentations to leadership/board
- **Lesson:** Prepare specific stories even if experience is adjacent

**3. Should Have Asked About Team Stability Earlier**
- Learned about 65% late delivery rate, distributed team challenges too late
- Would have influenced messaging if known upfront
- **Lesson:** Research deeper on operational health before interviews

**4. Mentioned Coyote (Minor)**
- Got distracted by backyard coyote during video interview
- Actually fine/humanizing, but stay focused on higher-stakes calls
- **Lesson:** Test tech, close windows, minimize distractions

#### Questions That Revealed Most

**To Katey (CSO):**
- "What did Amy Small do well, and what were the tensions?" → Revealed siloed team, performance orientation issues, nonprofit budget struggles

**To Ryan (EVP Client Services):**
- "What are your biggest delivery challenges?" → Revealed Giving Tuesday incident, resource constraints, need for nimbleness

**To Eric (CEO):**
- *(Didn't get to this stage but would have asked)* "Where does creative sit in your theory of change for clients?"

#### Their Questions That Mattered Most

**Katey:**
- "How do you balance emotional resonance with performance-oriented creative?" → They wanted both, not either/or
- "What's your AI perspective?" → Testing for thoughtfulness, not just adoption

**Ryan:**
- "How do you work with account managers and PMs?" → He manages them, wanted to know you won't create friction
- "Tell me about a time creative deliverables were delayed" → Testing for accountability and process

#### What Would Have Gotten You the Offer

Based on feedback (informal channel):
- **More new business experience** - They needed pitch expertise immediately
- **Agency background** - Nonprofit-to-agency transition felt like risk
- **Senior client relationship management** - Wanted someone who could be in the room with CMOs

#### What to Do Differently Next Time

**1. Front-load transition narrative**
- "I know I'm coming from nonprofit, here's why that's an asset not a gap..."
- Address in cover letter, not waiting for interview

**2. Translate experience more explicitly**
- Don't say "worked with program leads"
- Say "worked with program leads (analogous to account managers in agency world)"

**3. Ask about failure points earlier**
- "What's broken right now that this role needs to fix?"
- Better to know dealbreakers in screen call, not final round

**4. Prepare new business stories even if thin**
- Frame board presentations as pitches
- Discuss competitive scenarios (multiple agencies vying for partnership)
- Show you understand it's different from internal stakeholder management

### Universal Interview Principles

**First Impressions:**
- Join video 2 min early, test audio/video
- Background: bookshelf, plants, art (signals culture fit)
- Lighting: face clearly visible, not backlit
- Dress: One level up from company culture (business casual for nonprofits, blazer for corporate)

**Opening:**
- Have 60-second intro ready ("I've spent 9 years at PIH scaling creative operations...")
- Be ready for "Tell me about yourself" vs "Walk me through your resume" (different questions)
- Show energy, even on video (especially important when remote)

**During:**
- Use STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) but don't announce it
- Have 3-4 "go-to" stories that show different competencies
- Drop metrics naturally ("which led to 52% growth in digital fundraising")
- Ask for clarification if you need it ("Can you tell me more about what you mean by X?")

**Questions to Always Ask:**
1. "What does success look like in the first 90 days?"
2. "What's the biggest challenge facing the team/org right now?"
3. "Why is this role open / what happened with the previous person?"
4. "What do you most want the next person in this role to do differently?"
5. "What's your timeline for making a decision?"

**Questions to Avoid:**
- Anything you could have Googled (shows lack of preparation)
- Salary/benefits in first interview (too transactional)
- Softball questions ("What's a typical day like?")
- Complaints about current/former employer (unprofessional)

**Closing:**
- Restate enthusiasm: "I'm really excited about this opportunity because..."
- Clarify next steps: "What are the next steps in your process?"
- Ask for business card/email if not already connected
- Thank them, send follow-up within 24 hours

### Follow-Up Best Practices

**Timing:**
- After screen: 4-6 hours (shows promptness without desperation)
- After in-person: Same day, before 6pm (they'll check email before leaving)
- After final round: Within 12 hours (you're top of mind)

**Content:**
- Thank them for their time and specific conversation topics
- Reinforce 1-2 key points from the conversation
- Provide any materials they requested (portfolio links, references, etc.)
- Restate interest and fit
- End with clear next step or question

**Template:**
```
Subject: Thank you - [Your Name] - [Role Title] Interview

Hi [Name],

Thank you for taking the time to speak with me today about the [Role] position. I really enjoyed learning about [SPECIFIC THING from conversation].

[1-2 sentences reinforcing your fit based on what you learned in the interview]

[Optional: Provide additional context or material they asked for]

I'm excited about the opportunity to [SPECIFIC IMPACT you could have based on conversation] and look forward to the next steps in your process.

Best,
[Your name]
[Phone]
[LinkedIn]
```
